A note from the curator

This isn't a top-100-by-Metacritic list — the original Game Boy predates aggregate scores almost entirely, and a pure sales or review ranking would bury the system's real personality under six Mega Man ports and a dozen movie tie-ins. Instead, this collection samples every genre the DMG actually supported: the falling-block puzzlers it's remembered for, but also the SaGa-era Square RPGs, the Rare-built Donkey Kong Land trilogy, the Konami shooter output, and the Japan-only curios that never got a Western release at all.

Only games confirmed to run on original monochrome Game Boy hardware made the cut — Game Boy Color-exclusive titles (Pokémon Gold/Silver, Wario Land 3, Shantae, and similar) are deliberately excluded, even where they're beloved, because they simply won't boot on a stock grey brick.

Release years follow the North American release date where one exists. For games that never left Japan or only reached Japan and Europe, the original regional release year is used instead and the game is marked accordingly in its chapter.

A follow-up accuracy and regional-divergence pass corrected a few entries that had drifted from that convention or were mislabeled outright. Two games long assumed Japan-only — The Sword of Hope II and The King of Fighters '95 — actually did get delayed North American releases (1996 and 1997 respectively) and have been corrected accordingly; Trip World, previously listed as Japan-only, in fact reached Europe as well, just never North America. About a dozen entries also picked up a brief note on real European divergence — a different box title (Yoshi as Mario & Yoshi, Operation C as Probotector, Gradius: The Interstellar Assault as Nemesis II), or a release gap of many months to, in Wave Race's case, an extraordinary five years.
