A note from the curator

The original PlayStation closes out this project's four-platform expansion, and it's the collection where the games themselves finally get to lead every card. Earlier builds in this project treated regional release history as a running thread worth surfacing constantly; here, that's been dialled back on purpose. What makes a game worth including is what it actually did — a mechanic, a tone, a technical trick, a place in the console's history — and that comes first in every blurb. Regional detail only shows up where there's a real story attached to it: an actual title change, a genuine exclusivity, an unusual release order. A game that came out the same year everywhere, unremarkably, just doesn't get a regional mention at all.

That said, PS1's disc-based format did produce a real, striking pattern worth naming plainly: several Squaresoft RPGs from this era — Xenogears, Einhänder, Brave Fencer Musashi, and Threads of Fate among them — never received any European release at all, a gap that wouldn't repeat at the same scale on later Square platforms. A couple of games assumed to share that fate going in, Suikoden and Suikoden II, actually turned out to have released across all three regions just fine — a good reminder that an exclusivity assumption needs checking as carefully in either direction as anything else.

This build continues the tiered-research process from the previous three collections: well-established, unambiguous titles were drawn straight from existing knowledge, and dedicated research went specifically toward the roughly thirty games carrying a real regional-exclusivity claim, an unfamiliar credit, or a title that might differ by region.

With this collection, the project reaches its stated 12-platform goal.

Never released in Europe on PS1

  • Xenogears (1998)
  • Einhänder (1997)
  • Brave Fencer Musashi (1998)
  • Threads of Fate (1999)

Vib-Ribbon — the reverse case

  • Released in Japan and Europe in 1999, Vib-Ribbon never got a North American PS1 release at all — Sony reportedly passed on localizing it. North America finally got an official release 15 years later, via PS3 and PSN in 2014.
