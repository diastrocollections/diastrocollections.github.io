A note from the curator

The Game Boy Color gets treated by a lot of "best of" lists as an afterthought to the monochrome Game Boy or a warm-up act for the GBA — but it had a genuine identity of its own: a huge Pokémon-driven monster-collecting boom, Nintendo's first color Zelda duology, an unlikely run of real-time strategy experiments, and a deep well of Japan-only curios that never made it past import shops. This collection deliberately avoids re-listing anything already covered in the original monochrome Game Boy collection — everything here either requires color hardware outright or was built specifically to take advantage of it.

Release years follow the North American release date where one exists. For games that never left Japan or Europe, the original regional release year is used instead and the game is marked accordingly in its chapter — this collection leans on more of those than most, since a real chunk of the GBC's most interesting library never crossed the Pacific.

Every title below was individually fact-checked for release-platform accuracy — several were caught mid-draft as Game Boy Advance mix-ups or publisher/developer mix-ups and corrected before publication.

A short follow-up pass added further European context to this page after it first published. Where a PAL release carries a different box title, a real content difference, or a release gap of many months, that's folded directly into the relevant description below — Metal Gear: Ghost Babel's Japan-and-Europe-only bonus radio broadcast that North America's cartridge cut, Resident Evil Gaiden actually reaching Europe before it reached Japan or North America, or Pokémon Pinball's fifteen-month wait for a European release. It also turned up this platform's other, less-told story: a long list of games North America got that Europe simply never did, from Crystalis and Shantae to the English-language Dragon Warrior I & II and III remakes — now listed in their own section in the footer below, alongside the existing list of Japan/Europe-only titles that Europe (or Japan) got and North America didn't. Years above still generally mark the North American release; this is a targeted pass adding context, not a rebuild of the page's existing regional handling, which was already unusually thorough for a "best of" list.
