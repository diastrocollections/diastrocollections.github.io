A note from the curator

Microsoft's first console arrived loud: a black box built around a PC's guts, a controller the size of a dinner plate at launch, and a marketing pitch built entirely around horsepower Sony and Nintendo weren't offering yet. It launched in North America on 15 November 2001, then Japan on 22 February 2002, then Europe on 14 March 2002 — four months behind its own home debut, and the last of the three sixth-generation consoles to reach European shelves.

What Xbox actually turned out to be for wasn't raw power so much as two ideas nobody else had committed to yet: a shooter built specifically for a gamepad, and a built-in online service that worked. Halo did the first job so completely it's still the platform's whole identity, and Xbox Live did the second so well that Sony and Nintendo spent years catching up to it. Everything else here — the Splinter Cells and Ninja Gaidens and Steel Battalions — is Microsoft and its partners trying to fill in what a console built on those two ideas could also do.

A few of these hundred also appear in the PlayStation 2 or GameCube collections. That's allowed here where a game genuinely has a different story to tell on this console — extra content, a different guest character, a load-time advantage reviewers noted at the time — rather than just also existing elsewhere. This is the third and final of the new collections built alongside the original twelve, and Xbox closes it out the way it opened: unapologetically about the shooters, the horsepower, and the online play that got there first.

· Jesper

Worth knowing

  • Xbox launched in North America on 15 November 2001, Japan on 22 February 2002, and Europe on 14 March 2002 — four months behind its own home debut.
  • Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas's Xbox port ran with sharper textures and shorter load times than the original PlayStation 2 release, per reviews at the time.
  • Soulcalibur II's guest fighter changed by console: Spawn on Xbox, Link on GameCube, Heihachi on PlayStation 2.
  • Metal Gear Solid 2: Substance and Silent Hill 2: Restless Dreams both arrived on Xbox with bonus content missing from their original PlayStation 2 releases.
  • Project Zero II's Xbox Director's Cut added a first-person mode, a Survival mode and an alternate ending found nowhere else.
