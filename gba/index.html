A note from the curator

The Game Boy Advance has the deepest, most mainstream-friendly library of any platform in this series so far, which makes the temptation to just list a top-100-by-Metacritic even stronger — and even less interesting. This collection instead spreads across everything the GBA actually did well: Nintendo's own remasters and originals, four generations of Castlevania and Mega Man handheld spin-offs, an unusually strong run of Japan-only tactical and Summon-Night-style RPGs, several genuinely good movie and toy tie-ins, and a shooter chapter that punches well above the hardware's weight class.

Release years follow the North American release date where one exists. Several entries here have real gaps between their Japanese and North American release — sometimes by years, not months — and that gap is called out directly in the game's blurb or in the footer for anything that never left Japan at all.

Every title below was individually fact-checked for release-platform accuracy and developer attribution — the most common error caught and corrected was a publisher (Konami, Sega, Namco, Rockstar) being credited as the developer when a separate studio actually built the game.

A regional pass turned up its own quirks worth flagging: a run of Atlus-published RPGs (Riviera, the two Summon Night: Swordcraft Story games, Tactics Ogre: The Knight of Lodis) that reached Japan and North America but never got a European Game Boy Advance release at all; Yggdra Union, which only reached Europe in a limited print through select countries like Italy and France; Drill Dozer, which stayed Europe-less until a 2016 Wii U Virtual Console release; and Mr. Driller 2 and The Pinball of the Dead, both of which broke the usual Japan-first pattern — the former reached Europe over a year before North America, and the latter launched in North America a few weeks before Japan.
